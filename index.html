Agriculture · Honors Thesis

Algae Biostimulants in Strawberry Production

Independent, UF/IFAS-funded honors research · Advised by Dr. Xin Zhao · UF Horticultural Sciences Teaching Farm

I designed and led a split-plot field experiment testing an algae-based (Chlorella vulgaris) biostimulant on organic, high-tunnel strawberry production — across two cultivars and two fertigation rates. The study tracked leachate nutrient dynamics (NO₃⁻, K⁺, EC, pH), fruit yield and quality, and disease incidence over an 8-week harvest period, with results analyzed in SAS (PROC GLIMMIX) and R.

I also built an AI-assisted image-analysis phenotyping workflow in Fiji (ImageJ) to quantify leaf trichome density — pairing computational tools with traditional field research.

+36% yield in 'Sweet Sensation' with algae application
−29% yield in 'Florida Medallion' — same treatment

The cultivar-dependent split is the headline finding: biostimulant response isn't universal — it depends on genotype, and environmental stressors like frost events can outweigh treatment effects entirely. Presented at the UF Honors Thesis Symposium, Spring 2026.

Renewable Energy

Cyanothece & Supernatant Extraction

Undergraduate researcher, University of Florida

Working under a PhD student's guidance, I researched extraction methods for the supernatant of Cyanothece — a nitrogen-fixing cyanobacterium studied for its potential as a renewable, clean-energy feedstock. My focus was on finding more efficient supernatant extraction methods, a step toward making cyanobacteria-derived processes more viable at scale.

This work sits inside an active lab, so specifics stay high-level here — happy to discuss in more depth directly.

02 — Fashion & Sustainability

Where algae meets design

Runway experience meets a research background — looking at algae as material and inspiration.

Alongside my research, I've walked runway at New York Fashion Week. That experience, paired with a biology background, has pulled me toward the intersection of fashion and deep tech — most recently at the Sustainable Design Impact Hub's FashionXDeepTech event, where I heard from speakers working at that exact boundary.

I'm increasingly interested in algae as inspiration and material impact in fashion — from algae-based biotextiles and biopolymers to the pigments cultivated algae can produce as a lower-impact alternative to conventional dyes. It's an early, active interest rather than a finished portfolio, and it's the thread that ties my scientific work to a more creative practice.
